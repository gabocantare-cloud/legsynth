# Audit findings — legsynth, pre-publication

Written by the audit session. The session that fixes these has **no context from this
one**, so every finding below carries the file, the evidence, and the fix.

**State on arrival:** 91 tests passing, `ruff` clean, 8 commits on `main`, nothing
uncommitted, nothing pushed.

**Method.** No document in this repo was treated as evidence. `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`,
`docs/*.md` and every code comment are the *subject* of this audit, because the prose and
the `results/*.json` it cites were produced by the same AI session — confirming one
against the other proves nothing about either. Every number that costs less than a minute
was recomputed from `legsynth`. Campaign-scale numbers are labelled where they were
inherited from `results/*.json` rather than regenerated.

**Tooling written for this audit, and left in the repo:**

- `scripts/verify_docs.py` — 29 published numbers pinned to the exact sentence that
  quotes them, each recomputed from the package. `--slow` adds the claims inherited from
  `results/*.json`; `--coverage` lists numbers in the docs that no claim pins. **When you
  fix a number below, register the corrected value as a claim** so it cannot drift again -
  that is what the registry is for.
- `.claude/skills/audit-{numbers,code,repro,claims,portfolio,all}/` — the inspectors, so
  this audit is repeatable rather than a one-off.
- `results/audit/` — the twenty optimization campaigns this audit ran to settle §7.2, kept
  separate from `results/*.json` so the auditing session did not become a producer of the
  evidence it was auditing. It ships with the repo, so [E1](#e1) is checkable.

**Severity orders the work; it does not decide whether the work happens.** Everything
below is meant to be fixed. Where the fix is "delete or soften a sentence", that is the
recommendation, not a fallback.

## S1

### C1 — §7.1 states a caveat that §7.2, §7.3 and §7.4 then ignore {#c1}

**Where.** `docs/RESULTS.md` §7.1 (the caveat), §7.2, §7.3, §7.4 (the three uses).
Repeated in `CLAUDE.md` and `docs/DEFENDING_THIS.md`.

**What is wrong.** §7.1 measures the seed-to-seed spread from three campaigns and says so
plainly:

> three campaigns is a small sample from which to estimate a spread — this establishes
> that the gap is *of the same order* as the noise, not that it is provably zero.

That is the correct thing to say. The problem is that the same three-campaign spread is
then used as a precision instrument in three places, on differences of roughly its own
size:

- **§7.2** — "that change of 0.052 is **1.8 times the 0.0297 seed-to-seed spread**". A
  ratio quoted to two significant figures against a denominator §7.1 has just described
  as an order-of-magnitude statement.
- **§7.3** — "The 2.9% lost at 40° is smaller than the 3.5% the seed alone moves it, so
  it is not a cost at all." The 3.5% is `0.0024 / 0.0684`, the same spread. Two numbers
  0.6 percentage points apart are being used to decide free-versus-not-free.
- **§7.4** — the three band rows are one campaign each, and the improvement percentages
  (43.0 / 34.2 / 27.5) are read as a monotone trend with no error bar at all.

`spread()` in `scripts/robustness.py` is `max − min` over three values. The range of
three samples systematically *understates* dispersion, so every one of those comparisons
is biased toward "the difference is real".

**How this was established.** Read §7.1's caveat and §7.2/§7.3's usage together; confirmed
the arithmetic against `results/robustness.json` (`seed_spread_hypervolume` 0.00243,
`seed_spread_best_gait` 0.02970); confirmed `spread()` is a three-value range. Then ran
the replication in [E1](#e1) to find out whether the §7.2 gap actually holds up.

**Fix.** [E1](#e1) settled this by measuring the spread at ten triples instead of three,
and the answer was worse than this finding assumed: the hypervolume spread is **7.1x**
larger and the best-gait spread **2.7x** larger than §7.1 reports. So the fix is not a
caveat, it is a correction, and it splits three ways:

- **§7.2** — the conclusion is gone entirely. See [E1](#e1).
- **§7.3** — the design guideline is gone with it. See [C4](#c4).
- **§7.4** — the band study needs the caveat this finding originally proposed, and it is
  now a sharper one. The best-gait rows are 0.570 / 0.658 / 0.725, so consecutive bands are
  0.088 and 0.067 apart against a measured seed spread of **0.081** at the 1% band. The
  monotone trend, and the improvement percentages (43.0 / 34.2 / 27.5) read off it, are one
  campaign per row and are not separated from run-to-run variation. §7.4 does give a
  physical mechanism for the direction — a narrower band selects the flattest part of an
  already flat stroke — and that argument is unaffected. Keep the mechanism, keep the
  qualitative result (Jansen dominated at every band, 7/7, 20/20 and 15/15, which is far
  outside noise), and mark the magnitude trend as indicative rather than measured.

**Also fix `§7.1`'s spread table and the `§7` preamble** to the ten-triple numbers, since
every later section borrows that yardstick.

**Also fix `spread()`** in `scripts/robustness.py`. Returning `max − min` invites exactly
this error: the range of *n* samples grows with *n*, so the same function returns a
systematically smaller "spread" the fewer campaigns you run. Return the standard deviation
alongside it, and have the callers compare against that.

**Cost.** Prose plus a small code change. 0 min compute — [E1](#e1) already bought the
measurement.

### C2 — §7.2 rests on the one statistic §7's own preamble disqualifies {#c2}

**Where.** `docs/RESULTS.md` §7 preamble vs §7.2.

**What is wrong.** The preamble argues for hypervolume over best-gait-error, in these
words:

> It rewards a front for being both *good* … and *wide* …, and unlike "best gait error" it
> cannot be moved by one lucky design at one corner.

§7.2 then discards hypervolume — correctly, because the two campaigns' wear axes are not
comparable — and concludes on best gait error:

> the best achievable gait error moving from **0.658 to 0.710** … That is the finding, and
> it rests on the clean axis alone.

So the section's conclusion rests on precisely the statistic the section's methodology
paragraph called untrustworthy. Both halves were written by the same session and neither
noticed the other. A reviewer who reads §7 top to bottom will notice.

The finding is not necessarily wrong — §7.2's comparison is *paired* (same seed triple,
same band, same sample count, only `wear_key` changed), which is a genuinely stronger
design than the preamble's warning contemplates. But that argument is not made anywhere,
and it is the argument that rescues the section.

**How this was established.** Read the two paragraphs together. Confirmed the pairing in
`scripts/robustness.py:study_objective` — both campaigns use `SEED_TRIPLES[0]` and the
same `cfg`, and the cache key differs only in `wear_key`, so the two runs are as matched
as they can be.

**Fix.** [E1](#e1) resolved which side was right, and it was the preamble. Best gait error
*is* a single-corner statistic: at one pair it moved 0.052, and across ten pairs that
turned out to be the largest of ten values averaging −0.0036. Pairing was not enough to
rescue it.

So do not add a paragraph defending best gait error. Instead, in §7.2, say what actually
happened: hypervolume is unusable there because the two campaigns' wear axes differ, best
gait error is the only comparable axis, and it is too noisy at one pair to carry a
conclusion — which is why the section was re-run at ten. That turns an internal
contradiction into a worked example of the preamble's own warning, which is a better
section than the one there now.

**Cost.** Prose only. ~0 min compute.

### N2 — "0 of 600 satisfy the constraints" is one draw at one seed {#n2}

**Where.** `docs/RESULTS.md` §5, and repeated in `README.md:188`, `CLAUDE.md:142`,
`docs/NEXT_SESSION.md:181`, `docs/DEFENDING_THIS.md:200` and `:333`, and
`legsynth/optimize.py:233`.

**What is wrong.** §5 reports, in bold, that of 600 uniformly drawn designs, 103 (17%)
assemble and **0** satisfy the paper's constraints. Both numbers are properties of a
single random draw, presented as properties of the design box.

Re-running the same experiment at three seeds:

| seed | assemble | feasible |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 103 / 600 (17.2%) | **0** |
| 1 | 122 / 600 (20.3%) | **1** |
| 2 | 88 / 600 (14.7%) | **0** |

Seed 0 reproduces the published numbers exactly, so §5 was generated with
`default_rng(0)` and never re-drawn. The assembly rate is 14.7–20.3%, not 17%. And the
bold **0** — which is doing rhetorical work in six documents, and is the stated
justification for the seeded population — is 0 or 1 depending on the draw.

This matters more than its size suggests, because "0 of 600" is what licenses
`seeded_population`, and a seeded search is the repo's one documented deviation from the
paper. The deviation is still amply justified at 0–1 in 600 (under 0.2% feasible). The
claim just has to be true as stated.

The next sentence has the same problem: "Even jittering Jansen's own lengths by 2% leaves
only 4% of designs feasible." Measured at three seeds: **2.8%, 5.8%, 4.0%** — a spread of
2:1. The published 4% matches seed 2, while the 600-design figures match seed 0, so §5's
two sentences were not even generated from the same draw.

**How this was established.** Reproduced with `optimize.bounds()`, `optimize.evaluate()`
and `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)` for seeds 0, 1, 2 — about 40 s per seed for the
uniform draw, about 3 min per seed for the jitter variant.

**Fix.** Re-run at several seeds (see [R1](#r1), which needs the same script) and restate:
"14.7–20.3% assemble at all; 0 or 1 in 600 satisfies the paper's constraints, across three
independent draws" and "2.8–5.8% under a 2% jitter". Update all six copies, or better,
generate the sentence's numbers into `results/feasibility.json` and quote from one place.
Keep the conclusion — it is unchanged and now actually supported.

**Cost.** ~12 min compute for the re-draws (the jitter variant dominates); the rest is
prose.

### R1 — §5 is a published finding with no script behind it {#r1}

**Where.** `docs/RESULTS.md` §5; `scripts/reproduce.py`.

**What is wrong.** §5's numbers (600 designs, 17% assemble, 0 feasible, "even jittering
Jansen's own lengths by 2% leaves only 4% of designs feasible") exist in no script, no
test and no JSON file in this repo. `scripts/reproduce.py` does not produce them.
`grep -rn 600 scripts/ tests/` returns nothing. They were computed once in a session and
typed into a document.

Given that `.gitignore` keeps `results/*.json` out of git, a reader who clones this repo
cannot obtain §5's numbers by any documented route.

**How this was established.** `grep` across `scripts/` and `tests/`; read
`scripts/reproduce.py`'s stage list. Then reproduced §5 by hand, which is how [N2](#n2)
was found — the numbers are right for seed 0, but nothing in the repo says so.

**Fix.** Add `scripts/feasibility.py`: draw `n` designs uniformly from `optimize.bounds()`
at each of several seeds, report the assembly and feasibility rates, also do the 2%-jitter
variant, and write `results/feasibility.json`. Add it to `STAGES` in `reproduce.py` (it is
seconds, not minutes). Register its two headline numbers as claims in
`scripts/verify_docs.py`. Then rewrite §5 from its output.

**Cost.** ~30 min of writing, ~3 min compute.

### C3 — §7.1's verdict is a coin flip rendered as a binary {#c3}

**Where.** `scripts/robustness.py:study_seeds`, surfaced in `docs/RESULTS.md` §7.1.

**What is wrong.**

    verdict = "noise" if noise_hv >= gap_hv else "cost"

with `noise_hv = 0.00243` and `gap_hv = 0.00200`. A 0.0004 margin, from a three-sample
range, deciding a binary that the script then prints as "the constraint costs something;
the write-up must change". One more unconstrained campaign landing anywhere outside the
current 0.0660–0.0684 window can flip it in either direction.

§7.1's prose is more careful than its code — it leads with the *inspection* argument
(0.0664 falls inside 0.0660–0.0684, and 0.676 inside 0.658–0.688), which is the right
argument and does not depend on the margin. But the code emits a verdict, the JSON
records it, and a future session will read the verdict rather than the prose.

**How this was established.** Read `study_seeds`; arithmetic from
`results/robustness.json`.

**Update from [E1](#e1).** At ten triples the margin is no longer thin: the gap is 0.0020
against a spread of 0.0170, a factor of 8.5. **§7.1's verdict is correct and is now well
supported** — this finding is about the code emitting a fragile binary, not about the
answer being wrong.

**Fix.** Make `verdict` three-valued — `"noise"`, `"cost"`, and `"inconclusive"` when the
two quantities are within, say, 25% of each other — and record the margin in the JSON
alongside it. A fix session reading the JSON needs *something*, and an explicit
"inconclusive" is more useful than a coin flip rendered as a fact. Raise `SEED_TRIPLES` to
the ten triples [E1](#e1) ran, so a re-run reproduces the better spread rather than the
three-sample one.

**Cost.** ~15 min of code. No new compute for the verdict change; re-running
`robustness.py --study seeds` at ten triples would be ~30 min, and is optional because
`results/audit/objective_replication.json` already holds the campaigns.

### N3 — Ground clearance is an identity, published as a measurement {#n3}

**Where.** `legsynth/metrics.py:ground_clearance`; `docs/METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md:62` and
`:140`; `docs/RESULTS.md` §6 and §8.

**What is wrong.** With `y_ground = min(y) + band × H`:

    clearance = max(y) − y_ground = H − band × H = (1 − band) × H

Ground clearance is exactly 99% of path height at the published band. Verified:
22.232376 / 22.456946 = 0.9900000000, to ten decimal places, and the same ratio holds in
`results/clearance_fit.json`'s fitted design (25.714874 / 25.974620).

Nothing built on it is *wrong*. But three passages treat it as an independent observation
when it is arithmetic:

- METRIC_DEFINITIONS: "Clearance is bounded above by the total path height" — true, and
  much stronger than stated: it *equals* 0.99 of it.
- `metrics.py`: "it barely moves with the band — the one metric the stance definition
  cannot explain away." It moves *linearly and exactly* with the band, which is a
  stronger statement than "barely".
- RESULTS §6: "Every design on the front loses ground clearance, from 22.2 mm to roughly
  19 mm: nothing in either objective rewards lifting the foot higher." This is a claim
  about path height, and the 0.85× clearance constraint is a 0.85× path-height constraint.
  Same for §8: `clearance_fit.py` fitting clearance is fitting path height.

A mechanism-design reader will spot the identity in about a minute and wonder what else
was not reduced.

**How this was established.** Algebra, then confirmed numerically; claim `A1` in
`scripts/verify_docs.py` now pins the ratio to 1e-9 so it cannot silently stop being true.

**Fix.** State the identity once, in `docs/METRIC_DEFINITIONS.md` beside the formula table:
that at band `b` the clearance is exactly `(1−b) × H`, so it measures path height and the
band scales it linearly. Then adjust the three passages to say "path height" where that is
what they mean. No conclusion changes — §8's argument in particular gets *stronger*,
because "25.7 mm cannot come from a 22.46 mm path" becomes exact rather than a bound.

**Cost.** Prose only.

### K1 — `mean_force` is computed twice in `wear_per_cycle` {#k1}

`legsynth/wear.py:107-110`:

    mean_force = np.nanmean(f, axis=0) if np.isfinite(f).any() else np.full(10, np.nan)
    if not np.isfinite(f).all():
        mean_force = np.full(f.shape[1], np.nan)

The second statement overwrites the first in every case where the first did anything
interesting, so the `if/else` on line 107 is dead. Also note the hardcoded `10` in the
dead branch where the live one uses `f.shape[1]`.

Not a defect — behaviour is correct — but it means the block was edited without being
re-read, in the module that computes the repo's second objective. **Fix:** delete line
107's conditional, keep the guard.

### K2 — `check()` drops `band` {#k2}

`legsynth/constraints.py:213`: `ta = transmission_angles(leg, n)` — no `band`, so it
always uses `M.DEFAULT_BAND` regardless of what the caller is doing.

No current caller varies the band through `check()` (the optimizer path goes through
`optimize.describe`, which does pass `band` correctly), so nothing published is affected.
It is flagged because it is exactly the defect shape that already cost this repo one
study: a parameter threaded through one path and silently defaulted on another produced
plausible numbers rather than a crash, and `test_the_stance_band_reaches_the_statics_as_
well_as_the_metrics` exists because of it. **Fix:** give `check()` a `band` parameter and
pass it through; add it to that test's coverage.

### K3 — the same measurement at three sample counts {#k3}

`constraints.transmission_angles` defaults to `n=720`; `min_transmission_angle` defaults
to `n=720`; `check()` calls `transmission_angles(leg, n)` with its own `n=360` default;
`optimize.describe` calls it at `n=N_EVAL=360` in the search and `n=1440` when refining.
Four entry points, three answers, one name. Nothing published is wrong — the published
figures all come through `describe` at 1440 — but a reader comparing 8.6° from one call
site against a value from another will not get the same number. **Fix:** make the default
`M.N_PUBLISHED` everywhere, or document per-function why it differs.

### K4 — asymmetric edge mask in the virtual-work check {#k4}

`legsynth/dynamics.py:275`:

    edge |= np.roll(edge, -1) | np.roll(edge, 1) | np.roll(edge, 2)

drops one sample on one side of touchdown and two on the other. The residual is 4.1e-5
either way, so this changes nothing measurable, but an asymmetric window around a
symmetric discontinuity looks like a typo and will be read as one. **Fix:** make it
symmetric (`roll(-2)` as well) and re-check the residual, or add a comment saying why the
asymmetry is intended.

### P3 — a mean compared against a peak {#p3}

### R3 — CI gaps {#r3}

`.github/workflows/tests.yml` has never run (nothing has been pushed). Three things it
does not do:

- **It never runs `scripts/verify_docs.py`.** That is the whole point of having written
  it — the docs can drift from the code between now and the next commit. Add a step.
- **`ruff` is unpinned** (`pip install ruff`). The tree is clean on 0.16.6; a newer ruff
  with new default rules will fail the lint job on something cosmetic. Pin it.
- **`pyproject.toml` claims `requires-python = ">=3.9"` and CI tests 3.10 and 3.12.**
  The 3.9 floor is unverified. Either add 3.9 to the matrix or raise the floor to 3.10.

**Already fixed** (the one repo change this session made, on Gabriel's instruction):
`results/audit/` — where this audit's experiment output lives — was excluded by

### C4 — §7.3's design guideline compares against a yardstick 7x too small {#c4}

**Where.** `docs/RESULTS.md` §7.3, and the `figures/threshold_sweep.png` caption.

**What is wrong.** §7.3 converts the null result into a design rule:

> The 2.9% lost at 40° is smaller than the 3.5% the seed alone moves it (§7.1), so it is
> not a cost at all. The 11.1% lost at 45° is more than three times that spread, so it is.

Both halves lean entirely on the 3.5% figure, which is §7.1's three-triple hypervolume
spread expressed as a fraction (0.0024 / 0.0684). [E1](#e1) measured that spread at ten
triples: **0.0170, which is 24.9% of the same baseline**, not 3.5%.

Against the corrected yardstick:

| Threshold | hypervolume lost | vs. seed-alone movement (24.9%) |
|---|---|---|
| 40° | 3.0% | well inside noise |
| 45° | 11.1% | **inside noise** |
| 50° | 13.0% | inside noise |
| 55° | 100% | far outside noise |

So the sentence that makes §7.3 a *guideline* rather than a shrug — that 45° costs
something real — is not supported. Neither is the knee. The rows at 40°, 45° and 50° are
one campaign each at one seed triple, separated by less than the search's own run-to-run
variation.

**What survives, and it is not nothing.** Hypervolume falls monotonically across the four
thresholds, but that is guaranteed by the geometry — a tighter constraint can only shrink
the feasible set — so it is not evidence either way. **The 55° result is real and
dramatic:** 100% of the hypervolume lost, best gait error 2.343 against Jansen's 1.000,
nine feasible designs and not one that beats Jansen on both objectives. That is nowhere
near the noise floor and needs no defending. The observation that Jansen's own 42.7° sits
below the point where the constraint becomes impossible also survives.

**How this was established.** [E1](#e1)'s twenty campaigns, and arithmetic against the
threshold sweep in `results/robustness.json`.

**Fix.** Rewrite §7.3 around what is measured:

> Sweeping the threshold shows the constraint is free at 40° and impossible at 55°.
> Between those, at 45° and 50°, the hypervolume lost (11.1% and 13.0%) is smaller than
> the 24.9% the random seed alone moves it, so this sweep cannot say where the price
> begins — only that it is somewhere between 50° and 55°, and that Jansen's own 42.7° sits
> comfortably inside the free region.

Delete the one-line design guideline ("enforce 40° for free; expect to give up roughly a
tenth of the achievable improvement to reach 45°"). It quantifies a cost this data cannot
resolve. Keep the figure — the cliff is the point of it — and re-caption it to the
surviving claim.

**Cost to do better.** Repeating the sweep at ten seed triples per threshold is 40 more
campaigns, roughly **2 hours**. That would locate the knee properly, and it is the natural
follow-on to `docs/NEXT_SESSION.md` P2 #8, which already proposes sampling 41-44°. Worth
doing before anyone quotes the guideline in an interview; not worth blocking publication
on, because the softened wording above is fully supported today.

**Note which way this cuts.** It is the same error as §7.2 — one campaign compared against
an under-estimated spread — and it lands on the section `docs/NEXT_SESSION.md` P1 #5 calls
"the single most interesting plot in the repo". The plot is still interesting. The sentence
under it was doing more work than the data allows.

## E1 — the experiments this audit ran, and what they found {#e1}

**S1. §7.2's headline gap does not survive. And the noise yardstick §7.1 supplies to §7.2,
§7.3 and §7.4 is understated by a factor of seven.**

**The question.** §7.2 rests on one pair of campaigns at seed triple (0, 1, 2): best gait
error 0.658 under the paper's mean-force wear, 0.710 under the integrated form, a gap of
0.052 called "1.8 times the 0.0297 seed-to-seed spread". One pair, compared against a
spread measured in a different experiment at three campaigns.

**What was run.** Both objectives at ten seed triples — (0,1,2) through (27,28,29) —
identical settings throughout (`pop=100, gens=80`, refined at 1440, `min_angle=None`,
`band=0.01`). Six campaigns already existed in `results/robustness.json`; sixteen were run
here. **Twenty campaigns, 62 minutes on 12 cores.** Consolidated into
`results/audit/objective_replication.json`, with each campaign also appended to
`results/audit/objective_campaigns.jsonl` as it finished. Nothing in `results/*.json` or
`docs/` was touched.

### The paired result: the wear definition does not move the reachable gait quality

| Seed triple | best gait, mean-force | best gait, integrated | paired difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| (0, 1, 2) — §7.2's pair | 0.6580 | 0.7099 | **+0.0520** |
| (3, 4, 5) | 0.6659 | 0.6635 | −0.0024 |
| (6, 7, 8) | 0.6877 | 0.6977 | +0.0100 |
| (9, 10, 11) | 0.7150 | 0.6960 | −0.0190 |
| (12, 13, 14) | 0.7030 | 0.7050 | +0.0020 |
| (15, 16, 17) | 0.7389 | 0.7229 | −0.0160 |
| (18, 19, 20) | 0.6600 | 0.6640 | +0.0040 |
| (21, 22, 23) | 0.7000 | 0.6750 | −0.0250 |
| (24, 25, 26) | 0.7010 | 0.6790 | −0.0220 |
| (27, 28, 29) | 0.6930 | 0.6740 | −0.0190 |

**Mean paired difference −0.0036, sd 0.0231, standard error 0.0073, 95% CI
[−0.0179, +0.0107].** Four of ten are positive. The interval straddles zero and the point
estimate has the *opposite sign* to the published claim.

**The published +0.0520 is the maximum of all ten.** It is not a small sample of a real
effect; it is the extreme tail of a distribution centred on zero. §7.2 published the one
pair in ten that supports its conclusion, having drawn only that one.

**What survives.** Jansen stays Pareto-dominated under either wear definition, now at ten
triples rather than one. The §3 finding — that the mean-force shortcut overstates absolute
wear by 51.3% — is a separate measurement on the Jansen baseline and is untouched.

**What does not survive.** "The front moves, and it moves by more than the noise"; "1.8
times the seed-to-seed spread"; and the conclusion that the shortcut "moves where the
optimum is". At ten paired triples, switching which wear number is minimised does not
detectably change the reachable gait quality.

### The by-product: §7.1's spread is far too small

Twenty campaigns give the seed-to-seed spread §7.1 estimated from three. The comparison is
not close:

| | §7.1, 3 triples | measured, 10 triples | understated by |
|---|---|---|---|
| hypervolume, range | 0.0024 | **0.0170** (sd 0.0052) | **7.1x** |
| best gait error, range | 0.0297 | **0.0810** (sd 0.0255) | **2.7x** |

This is the max-minus-min problem [C1](#c1) predicted, and it is worse than expected. The
range of three samples has an expected value of about 1.7 standard deviations; the range of
ten, about 3.1. Three campaigns did not merely give a noisy estimate of the spread — they
gave one biased low by construction.

Every comparison in §7.2, §7.3 and §7.4 that uses "the seed-to-seed spread" as a yardstick
is measuring against a number several times too small. That is [C1](#c1)'s concern, now
quantified, and it is what makes [C4](#c4) an S1 rather than a caveat.

### §7.1's own conclusion gets stronger, not weaker

Re-checked at ten triples, the inspection argument §7.1 leads with holds with far more room
than before:

- constrained-40° hypervolume **0.0664** sits inside the unconstrained range
  **0.0514-0.0684**
- constrained-40° best gait **0.676** sits inside **0.658-0.739**

And the margin [C3](#c3) worried about is gone: the gap is 0.0020 against a spread of
0.0170, a factor of 8.5 rather than 1.2. **§7.1's verdict — that the residual gap between
the constrained and unconstrained fronts is search noise rather than a cost — is now
solidly established.** The negative result is the best-supported thing in §7.

### Fix for §7.2

Rewrite the verdict to what ten pairs support:

> Switching the second objective from the paper's mean-force wear to the integrated form
> does not detectably change the reachable gait quality. Across ten seed triples the paired
> difference in best gait error averages −0.0036 (95% CI −0.018 to +0.011), with four of
> ten positive. The paper's central claim survives under either definition: Jansen is
> Pareto-dominated on every front measured. The first finding about the shortcut — that its
> absolute wear figures are high by 51% — is a separate measurement and stands.

Then propagate. Four documents assert the strong version:

- `docs/RESULTS.md` §7.2 — the verdict, and the "What survives and what does not" list,
  whose second bullet ("'The bias partly cancels' does not survive") is now itself refuted.
- `CLAUDE.md` — "moves the best achievable gait error from 0.658 to 0.710, which is 1.8x
  the seed-to-seed spread."
- `docs/NEXT_SESSION.md` ground rules — "Do not restore 'the wear bias cancels in the
  ratio'. §7.2 measured it... the optimum moves by 1.8x the seed-to-seed spread."
- `docs/DEFENDING_THIS.md` Finding 2 — "The shortcut does not merely inflate the wear
  figures by half, it moves the optimum", and the "mostly, but not enough" line offered as
  the interview answer.

**On the direction of the correction.** Ten paired triples centred on zero are much closer
to evidence *for* "the bias largely cancels in the ratio" than the old single pair was
against it. But the interval still admits ±0.018, so the honest statement is "no detectable
effect at ten triples", not "proven to cancel". The `NEXT_SESSION.md` ground rule should
become: *the objective-swap comparison was measured at ten seed triples and came back null;
do not re-assert movement of the optimum without new evidence.*

**Also update §7.1's spread table** to the ten-triple numbers, and the §7 preamble, so the
yardstick every later section borrows is the honest one. `results/audit/` ships with the
repo, so the twenty campaigns behind these numbers are checkable.
